- **Research problem:** existing multi-step conformal methods either calibrate each horizon independently, ignoring temporal correlation between horizons, or enforce strict simultaneous coverage and become over-conservative.
- **Method:** a unified online conformal framework that models multi-step error dependence explicitly while retaining long-run marginal coverage. It first proves that standard online conformal updates stay valid as long as the calibration parameters remain inside a defined "safe" region, then adds an online update step plus an optimisation step capturing inter-step dependence. Produces intervals around sampled trajectories, and can produce discontinuous intervals.
- **Datasets:** trajectory-ensemble settings — autonomous driving, hurricane forecasting, epidemic modelling. **Not the LTSF suite.**
- **Relevance to OUR wedge:** closest to **W3** (marginal versus joint whole-path coverage), not W2. It attacks the same tension we quantify — independent-per-horizon calibration versus simultaneous coverage — but on trajectory ensembles at short horizons, with no channel conditioning, no LTSF backbones, and no 96–720 grid. Our W3 contribution is the *price*: whole-path coverage on the LTSF grid costs ≈10× width (MaxScore 0.944 at H=96, decaying to 0.842–0.895 at H=720) while every per-step method sits at 0.000.
